As an engineer accelerating reacting-flow simulations, I want a learned source-term surrogate inside a classical solver, so that stiff chemistry can run faster without asking a network to replace transport, conservation, or the whole PDE solve.

The surrogate MUST replace only the chemistry integration substep. The surrounding classical solver MUST remain responsible for transport, boundary conditions, time integration, and conservation, and the surrogate MUST NOT be presented as a replacement for the full simulator.

Reference data MUST be generated by a named stiff integrator with the chemical mechanism, solver version, tolerances, state variables, physical units, thermodynamic constraints, sampled operating envelope, and measured stiffness ratio recorded. Training, validation, and test conditions MUST be separated before model development.

The evaluation MUST compare the surrogate against the competently tuned stiff integrator and against the practical incumbent, such as ISAT or an available tabulation or manifold method, at matched error. It MUST report both isolated chemistry acceleration and end-to-end simulation acceleration, and MUST NOT turn an ML-versus-ML comparison into a solver-speedup claim.

Every predicted state MUST satisfy the declared physical admissibility checks, including elemental balance, nonnegative species mass fractions, normalized composition, and thermodynamic consistency. A state outside the trained envelope or failing an admissibility check MUST fall back to the classical chemistry integrator rather than being silently accepted.

The evaluation MUST include long integrations and the steepest reaction-rate cases required by the application, and MUST report ignition delay, heat release, temperature, species errors, conservation errors, fallback rate, and the first point at which a trajectory becomes unacceptable. Single-step mean error MUST NOT stand in for rollout stability.

Never tune the surrogate against the held-out test conditions or weaken the classical baseline after seeing the surrogate result.

For example, a hydrogen-air ignition benchmark spanning the temperature, pressure, equivalence-ratio, and reaction-rate scales that caused the original PINN gradients to diverge must compare the learned chemistry flow map with the same CVODE or Radau configuration used to generate truth and with the available tabulation baseline. A fast prediction that violates elemental balance or misses ignition delay must be rejected and routed through the classical integrator.
